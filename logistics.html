Practical stuff

Flights, cars, SIMs, budget.

The handful of things worth booking before you fly — and roughly what it all cost.

Getting there

Flights

From the US, the two practical paths are via Europe (London, Amsterdam, Frankfurt — usually British Airways, KLM, Lufthansa) or, since 2021, United's direct Newark–Cape Town and Newark–Johannesburg flights. Plan ~28–36 hours door-to-door no matter what.

Fly into Joburg (JNB), out of Cape Town (CPT) — or vice versa — so you don't backtrack. The internal JNB–CPT segment is a 2-hour hop on British Airways or FlySafair for ~$80.

Safari connections

Airlink runs the small turbo-prop hops to the safari airports — Hoedspruit (HDS) for the western Kruger lodges, Skukuza (SZK) for central Kruger, Kasane (BBK) for Chobe. They handle the lodge transfers automatically once you book.

The lodge itself usually arranges your road transfer from the local airport — you'll get pickup instructions a few weeks before the trip.

Getting around

Rental car (Garden Route)

The Garden Route is a road trip — rent a car in Cape Town, drop it back in Cape Town a week later. The N2 east is well-paved and easy in any two-wheel-drive. Allow 5–6 hours Cape Town–Knysna and another 1 hour to Plett.

South Africa drives on the left. Most rentals are manual; specify automatic when booking. Avis, Europcar, and Bidvest have desks at CPT airport and in the CBD.

Cape Town: car vs. Uber

In the city itself, Uber is cheap and ubiquitous — a Camps Bay ↔ CBD ride is usually under R150 (~$8). For Cape Point, Stellenbosch, and the winelands, hire a driver or join a tour — you don't want to be the designated driver after a tasting day.

Joburg: do not walk

Stay in Sandton or Rosebank, use Uber for everything, don't walk around at night. The hotels are clustered close to the dining and Sandton City mall — you barely need transport beyond airport pickup.

Connectivity

SIM card or eSIM

In 2017 we picked up MTN tourist SIMs at CPT arrivals — R200 for a few GB. Worked everywhere with coverage, including Camps Bay and the Garden Route. No signal on the Ngala concession — that's by design.

These days an eSIM from Airalo or Ubigi is faster and cheaper — install before you fly, activate on landing. 5GB for 30 days runs $15–25.

Power, plugs, water

South Africa uses a unique three-pin (Type M) plug, sometimes Type N or even European Type C. Buy a multi-region adapter; the airport convenience stores also sell them.

Tap water is potable in Cape Town and the major cities, but load shedding (rolling blackouts) is a fact of life — check the Eskom schedule before assuming your hotel will have power at 7pm. Most decent hotels have backup generators or inverters.

Roughly what it cost

The 2017 trip ran ~$10,200 for two people over 14 days — or ~$5,100 per person — pre-booked, before food and shopping. The safari is the dominant line item: ~$1,000–$1,200 per person per night at a top-tier private lodge, all-inclusive of game drives, food, and most drinks. Cape Town and the Garden Route are remarkably cheap by US standards — the strong dollar versus the rand more than makes up for the long flights.

A few practical notes

Vaccinations & malaria

The Cape and Garden Route are malaria-free. The Kruger area is malaria zone — check with a travel doctor about prophylaxis (most opt for Malarone). Hep A, typhoid, and a current tetanus booster are standard recommendations. No yellow fever required from the US.

Cash, cards, tipping

Most restaurants, hotels, and shops take cards. Pull cash at any major bank ATM (Standard Bank, FNB, Absa). Tipping is real — 10–15% in restaurants, R10–20 for petrol attendants and car guards, and a meaningful tip to your safari ranger and tracker at the end of the stay (often US$10–20 per guest per day).

When to go

October–April is summer in the southern hemisphere — warm and dry on the Cape, hot and green in the Kruger (great game viewing in the dry-season tail of Sep–Oct, lush and stormy in Dec–Feb). November is a sweet spot: shoulder season at the lodges, warm but not yet packed in Cape Town, and southern right whales still around Hermanus.
