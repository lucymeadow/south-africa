Garden Route

A road trip east from Cape Town · 2 nights through L'Agulhas, Knysna, Plett.
Tuinroete

Click any pin for the note and links. see the full trip map →

Rental car required N2 east from Cape Town Drive on the left Allow a long day each way

The Garden Route is the classic coastal road trip east from Cape Town — some 200 km of forested mountain, lagoon, and cliffs along the southern Cape coast. Officially it runs from Mossel Bay to Storms River; we drove the backbone of it, stopping in Knysna and Plettenberg Bay, with the optional southern detour to L'Agulhas — the actual tip of the African continent — on the way out.

A two-night Garden Route trip is the minimum that feels worth it. Three or four nights gives you proper time for the wildlife sanctuaries, more of the hiking at Robberg or Tsitsikamma, and an afternoon in the indigenous forest.

The drive

Cape Town → Knysna, the scenic version

Cape Town → L'Agulhas (~3 hr, via the N2 then south on the R316) for a quick stop at the official southern tip of Africa.
L'Agulhas → Mossel Bay (~3 hr, back to the N2 east). Lunch at the Sea Shack on the dunes.
Mossel Bay → Knysna (~1.5 hr) on the N2 through the forest.
Total: ~8 hours of driving plus stops. Leave early.

Where we stayed

  • Stay

    Augusta Bay of Knysna

    Small guest house perched above Knysna Lagoon with views to the Knysna Heads. Breakfast in the central atrium, walking distance to the Knysna Quays.

  • Stay

    Plett Beachfront Accommodation

    Self-catering apartments steps from Central Beach in Plettenberg Bay. Walk to dinner, walk to the beach, that's the brief.

Knysna

  • See

    Knysna Heads

    Two sandstone cliffs that form the dramatic mouth of Knysna Lagoon — one of the most photographed estuaries in the country. Drive out to the East Head viewpoint and look back into the lagoon. The narrow channel between the heads is so treacherous it's considered un-navigable by larger boats.

  • See

    Margaret's Viewpoint

    Quiet pullout above Knysna with a sweeping view down the lagoon — better than the postcards, and almost always empty.

  • Eat · $$

    East Head Café

    Brunch institution literally cantilevered over the Heads. Order Knysna oysters — they're local, briny, and cheap. The view is the meal.

  • Eat · $

    Freshline Fisheries

    Working fish co-op at the Knysna Waterfront — paper cones of fresh calamari or hake-and-chips, eat on the dock.

  • Eat · $$

    JJ's Restaurant

    Dinner spot on the Knysna Quays — steaks, seafood, candlelit waterfront tables.

Plettenberg Bay

  • See

    Robberg Nature Reserve

    Five-hour clifftop loop on a rocky peninsula outside Plett — dolphins below, fur seal colony at the point, fynbos and sea everywhere. The signature Garden Route hike. Pack water, lunch, and good shoes. Watch the tide at the sand-bar crossing.

  • Do

    Monkeyland & Birds of Eden

    Free-roaming primate sanctuary and the world's largest free-flight aviary, side by side in the Crags outside Plett. Tickets cover both; the guided forest walks take ~2–3 hours total.

  • Do

    Jukani Wildlife Sanctuary

    Big-cat sanctuary on the same N2 stretch — lions, tigers, leopards, cheetahs rescued from the wildlife trade. Conservation-first ethic, no cub petting. Combine all three sanctuaries on a single Plett afternoon.

  • Eat · $$

    The Lookout Deck

    Open-deck restaurant on the Plett headland with a 180-degree bay view. The spot for whale-watching with a glass of wine in your hand (Jun–Nov for southern right whales).

  • Eat · $$

    Emily Moon River Lodge

    African-fusion restaurant on a wooden deck over the Bitou River, just inland of Plett. Eclectic decor, very long sunsets.

  • Eat · $

    Clare's Cakes & Coffee

    Tiny Plett bakery for a breakfast pastry and a packed lunch box to carry on the Robberg hike.

Quick detours

  • See

    L'Agulhas (southern tip of Africa)

    The actual southernmost point of the continent — where the Atlantic and Indian Oceans officially meet. A marker stone, a 19th-century lighthouse, and not much else; that's the point. Worthwhile detour on the way out of Cape Town.

  • Eat · $

    Sea Shack Struisbaai

    Beach shack right on the dunes outside L'Agulhas — fresh fish and chips at picnic tables in the sand. The lunch stop on the drive east.

If you have an extra night or two

Tsitsikamma National Park (45 min east of Plett) for the suspension bridge and indigenous forest hikes — sleep in one of the SANParks chalets or at the Tsitsikamma Village Inn.

Bloukrans Bridge bungee jump (216 m) is in the same stretch — the world's highest bridge jump if you're into that kind of holiday.

Hermanus (1.5 hr east of Cape Town) for southern right whale watching off the cliffs, Jun–Nov. The "whale crier" walks the cliff with a kelp horn.
