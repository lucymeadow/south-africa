Greater Kruger Private concession Big Five Malaria zone All-inclusive

The safari was the reason for the trip and remains the highlight years later. We stayed at &Beyond Ngala, a 20-cottage thatched lodge in the &Beyond Ngala Private Game Reserve. It shares an unfenced 14,700-hectare border with Kruger National Park, meaning the animals move freely through it — and the rangers can off-road for sightings the way they can't in the public park.

Where we stayed

  • Stay

    &Beyond Ngala Safari Lodge

    Classic-colonial thatched lodge under mopane and tamboti trees. Twenty air-conditioned safari cottages with floor-to-ceiling windows and a private veranda each, a pool overlooking riverine woodland, an open boma for fire-lit dinners. The ranger-and-tracker pairings are excellent — ask about a walking safari on at least one morning.

What a day looks like

The Ngala daily rhythm

5:30am — gentle knock-and-coffee wake-up.
6:00am — depart on morning game drive (rusks & coffee on the vehicle).
~10:00am — return, full brunch served outside.
Midday — nap, pool, spa, sit on the veranda and watch the waterhole.
3:30pm — high tea, then depart on afternoon drive.
Sundowners — gin & tonics from the cooler box, watching the light leave.
After dark — the drive continues with spotlights for nocturnal sightings.
7:30pm — multi-course dinner in the courtyard, the boma, or the bush.

What you'll likely see

  • Wildlife

    The Big Five · and most of the rest

    Four nights at Ngala typically gets you all of the Big Five — lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo, rhino — plus the supporting cast: giraffe, zebra, kudu, impala, hyena, jackal, hippo and crocodile at any water, plus a dozen other antelope. Leopard sightings here are unusually reliable. Wild dog and cheetah are bonus animals.

Getting there

  • Do

    Hoedspruit Airport (HDS)

    Tiny gateway airport in Limpopo — one-hour Airlink hop from O.R. Tambo, then a ~45-minute &Beyond road transfer to Ngala. Open-air arrivals, a literal thatched roof. The trip basically starts the moment you land.

Things worth knowing before you book

Top safari lodges book 6–12 months out, especially Jun–Oct (dry season, peak game viewing) and the December holidays. Nov was a good shoulder window — warm, lush, smaller crowds.

The Kruger area is a malaria zone. Talk to a travel doctor 6+ weeks before departure about prophylaxis — most opt for Malarone.

Pack light, in earth tones. Most lodges have free laundry. Loose long sleeves and pants for early morning and dusk; layers for sunset.

Tip your ranger and tracker. US$10–20 per guest per day is the standard.
