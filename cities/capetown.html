Cape Town is the anchor of any first South Africa trip. A walkable beach strip on the Atlantic, a flat-topped mountain rising straight out of the city, an hour from the winelands, an hour from a wild peninsula, and a working ferry to Mandela's prison. We based both halves of our Cape Town time in Camps Bay — right on the beach, with the Twelve Apostles range behind — and used Uber for everything in town.

Where we stayed

  • Stay

    Primi Seacastle

    Tiny boutique hotel right on the Camps Bay beach strip — Atlantic surf outside the window, the Twelve Apostles behind. First-leg base; easy walking to the strip's bars and restaurants.

  • Stay

    Fairways On The Bay

    Eight-room guesthouse a hundred meters from Camps Bay Beach. Quieter than the strip itself, with proper rooms and a small pool. Used as the second Cape Town base after the Garden Route loop.

Sights worth your time

  • See

    Table Mountain

    The flat-topped icon. Take the cableway up on a clear morning or hike one of the routes from Kirstenbosch. The famous "tablecloth" of cloud can roll in any afternoon — if it's clear at 8am, go.

  • Do

    Skeleton Gorge / Nursery Ravine Hike

    The classic Table Mountain hike from the back, starting in Kirstenbosch. 5–6 hours up Skeleton Gorge and down Nursery Ravine. Go with a guide from Hike Table Mountain — they know the weather windows and which route to switch to if the wind picks up.

  • See

    Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden

    One of the world's great botanical gardens, on the eastern slopes of Table Mountain. The Boomslang treetop canopy walk, the proteas, the tea room. In summer (Nov–Mar) there are open-air Sunday sunset concerts.

  • See

    Cape of Good Hope & Cape Point

    The southwestern tip of Africa. Best as a full-day private loop down the peninsula with a local driver — Chapman's Peak Drive, the Cape itself, the Boulders penguins, lunch in Simon's Town. We used Lynette at Exquisite Cape Tours and the day she ran was exceptional.

  • See

    Boulders Penguin Colony

    A wild colony of ~2,000 African penguins on a beach in Simon's Town. Combine with the Cape Point loop — they're 20 minutes apart.

  • See

    Robben Island

    The prison where Nelson Mandela spent 18 of his 27 years. ~3.5-hour ferry-and- tour from the V&A Waterfront, guided by a former political prisoner. Book ahead — weekday tours fill a week out.

  • See

    V&A Waterfront

    Working harbour redeveloped into Cape Town's main eating, shopping, and ferry hub. The V&A Food Market is a quick lunch; the Zeitz MOCAA contemporary-art museum in the old grain silo is worth an hour, particularly for the architecture by Heatherwick.

  • See

    Lion's Head

    Conical peak next to Table Mountain — a shorter hike than the main mountain, with 360-degree views. Locals do it for sunrise or for full-moon sunsets; bring a headlamp and join the procession.

Neighborhoods to walk

  • Neighborhood

    Bo-Kaap

    Cape Malay neighborhood of brightly painted cobblestone streets on the slopes of Signal Hill. Walk it on a Sunday morning; the Bo-Kaap Museum tells the story behind the color, and several locals run small guided walking tours.

  • Neighborhood

    Camps Bay & Clifton

    The Atlantic Seaboard — white-sand beaches under the Twelve Apostles range, palm-lined promenade, sundowner bars from one end to the other. Camps Bay is louder and more touristy; the four Clifton coves next door are quieter and prettier.

Where we ate (and drank)

  • Eat · $$

    Kloof Street House

    Victorian house turned colonial-trendy restaurant on Kloof Street. Outdoor deck under fairy lights, library room with a fireplace, eclectic seasonal menu. A perfect first-night Cape Town dinner.

  • Eat · $$

    The Codfather

    Camps Bay seafood and sushi institution — you walk in, pick your fish off the ice with the chef, they cook it however you ask. Loud, casual, fun.

  • Eat · $$$

    La Colombe

    Tasting-menu fine dining in a forest setting in Constantia. Consistently in the country's top 3 restaurants; the cape-malay-influenced menu is genuinely inventive. Long-lunch territory; book months ahead.

  • Eat · $$$

    The Pot Luck Club

    Luke Dale Roberts's small-plates spot at the top of the silo at the Old Biscuit Mill, Woodstock. 360 city views, tasting menu of five-flavor categories. Book weeks ahead and pair with a morning at the Saturday Neighbourgoods Market downstairs.

  • Eat · $$$

    Azure at the Twelve Apostles

    Cliffside restaurant at the Twelve Apostles Hotel, between Camps Bay and Llandudno. The drive down the coast at sunset is half the experience.

  • Eat · $$

    Savoy Cabbage

    Long-running farm-to-table bistro in the CBD — known for the tomato tart and the ostrich carpaccio.

  • Eat · $

    Eastern Food Bazaar

    Cheap-and-cheerful pan-Asian food hall on Longmarket Street — biryani, curry, dosas, kebabs from a dozen counters. Easy lunch break in the CBD.

  • Drink

    The Bungalow

    Glass-front beach club at Clifton 3, with a wraparound deck overhanging the Atlantic. The food is fine; the view is the point. Sundowners on the deck is the move — reserve.
