Joburg is the international gateway: most flights into South Africa land at O.R. Tambo (JNB), and most safaris connect through here. We used it as a sleep-and-dinner stop either side of the bush. Stay in Sandton — the modern business district with hotels, restaurants, and Nelson Mandela Square clustered close together. The rest of Joburg is fascinating (Constitution Hill, Apartheid Museum, Soweto) and worth a real visit on a future trip; this guide is just the transit version.

Where we stayed

  • Stay

    The Capital Empire

    Apartment-style hotel in Sandton, ~20 minutes from O.R. Tambo and walking distance to Nelson Mandela Square. Quiet, modern rooms, useful kitchenettes if your flight lands at a weird hour. Same hotel both stops.

Things to do (in transit)

  • See

    Nelson Mandela Square

    Open plaza in Sandton with the famous 6-meter bronze of Madiba and the entrance to the Sandton City mall. Useful early-evening walk before dinner; both Bukhara and The Butcher Shop & Grill open onto the square.

Where we ate

  • Eat · $$

    Bukhara

    Long-running Indian restaurant on Nelson Mandela Square — tandoor lamb, black daal, a serious wine list, generous portions. The best easy first-night dinner after the long flight in.

  • Eat · $$$

    The Butcher Shop & Grill

    Old-school steakhouse on the square. South Africa is a beef country, and this is where you remind yourself of that fact — dry-aged ribeye, a heavy red, no apologies. Booking helps.

If you have a real day in Joburg

We didn't have time on this trip, but the things to add for a 24–48 hour visit are the Apartheid Museum, Constitution Hill, and a guided Soweto tour that includes Vilakazi Street (the only street in the world to have housed two Nobel laureates, Mandela and Tutu). They're the reason to spend a proper day here.
